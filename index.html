Lesson 01

Aperture & Depth of Field (Bokeh)

Aperture is the diaphragm opening inside your lens (measured in f-stops like f/1.4, f/2.8, f/16). It controls two crucial properties:

  • Amount of Light: Lower f-number = wider opening = more light.
  • Depth of Field (DoF): A wide aperture (f/1.4 - f/2.8) isolates the subject with a blurred background (bokeh). A narrow aperture (f/8 - f/16) keeps everything in focus from front to back.
Formula: F-Number N = Focal Length (f) / Aperture Diameter (D)
Lesson 02

Shutter Speed & Motion Control

Shutter speed is the duration the camera sensor is exposed to light (e.g. 1/4000s to 1 full second).

  • Freezing Action (1/500s - 1/4000s): Sharpens fast moving objects like spinning wheels, droplets, or athletes.
  • Motion Blur (1/30s - 1s+): Creates artistic streaks, silk water, and speed sensations. Requires a tripod or stabilization!
Rule of Thumb: Minimum handheld speed ≈ 1 / Focal Length (e.g., 50mm → 1/50s)
Lesson 03

ISO Sensitivity & Sensor Grain

ISO amplifies the electronic signal gathered by your sensor pixels. It allows shooting in dark environments without a flash:

  • Base ISO (100 - 200): Purest image quality, maximum dynamic range, and zero digital grain.
  • High ISO (1600 - 12800): Brightens dark scenes but introduces luminance and chroma noise (grain), reducing fine sharpness.
Concept: Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) decreases as ISO gain increases
Lesson 04

The Exposure Triangle & EV Scale

Exposure Value (EV) measures the total light hitting the sensor. Changing one setting requires compensating with another to maintain balanced exposure (0 EV):

  • 1 stop faster shutter (e.g., 1/125s → 1/250s) cuts light in half (-1 EV).
  • Compensate by opening aperture 1 stop (f/4 → f/2.8) or doubling ISO (200 → 400).
EV Formula: EV = log₂(N² / t) - log₂(ISO / 100)
Lesson 05

Focal Length & Perspective Field of View

The focal length (in mm) determines your angle of view and visual compression:

  • Wide-Angle (18mm - 35mm): Expansive field of view, ideal for architecture and sweeping horizons.
  • Standard (50mm): Natural perspective closely mimicking the human eye's single-glance perspective.
  • Telephoto (85mm - 200mm): Compresses background distances and renders flattering, distortion-free portraits.
FOV Formula: 2 × arctan(Sensor Width / (2 × Focal Length))
Lesson 06

Sensor Sizes & The Crop Factor

Digital sensors vary in physical surface area compared to classic 35mm film (Full Frame):

  • Full Frame (1.0x): 36x24mm standard. Largest pixels, maximum light gathering.
  • APS-C (1.5x): Cropped sensor. A 50mm lens behaves with the field of view of a 75mm lens (50 × 1.5).
  • Micro Four Thirds (2.0x): 2x crop factor. 50mm becomes equivalent to 100mm telephoto.
Equivalent Focal Length = Physical mm × Crop Factor

Bokeh

The aesthetic quality of the out-of-focus blur in parts of an image produced by an optical lens. Rendered smoothly by circular aperture diaphragms.

Circle of Confusion (CoC)

An optical measurement defining the size of a point of light on the sensor before the human eye perceives it as unsharp or blurred.

Exposure Value (EV)

A logarithmic scale combining shutter speed and f-number. EV 0 represents an exposure of 1 second at f/1.0 with ISO 100.

Hyperfocal Distance

The focus distance that yields the greatest depth of field, keeping everything sharp from half the hyperfocal distance to infinity.

Sunny 16 Rule

A classic exposure rule stating that on a clear sunny day, an aperture of f/16 will yield correct exposure with a shutter speed equal to 1 / ISO.

Pinhole Projection

The fundamental geometric projection model of cameras, mapping 3D space $(X, Y, Z)$ to 2D pixels $(u, v)$ via perspective division.

The Mathematics of Camera Models

A camera model is a mathematical description of the process of projecting 3D points in the physical world onto a 2D digital sensor plane.

1. The Pinhole Camera Model

In a standard pinhole camera, light rays pass through a single point (the optical center or projection center) and form an inverted image on the sensor plane.

u = f * (X / Z) + u₀
v = f * (Y / Z) + v₀

Where:

  • (X, Y, Z): 3D point coordinates in the camera coordinate frame (Z is distance from lens).
  • f: Focal length of the lens.
  • (u, v): 2D pixel coordinates on the digital sensor.
  • (u₀, v₀): Principal point (optical center of the digital sensor).

2. Camera Intrinsic Matrix (K)

The intrinsic matrix handles parameters specific to the camera hardware (focal length, sensor aspect ratio, and optical center shifts):

[ u ]   [ f_x   s   u₀ ] [ X ]
[ v ] = [ 0   f_y   v₀ ] [ Y ]
[ 1 ]   [ 0   0   1 ] [ Z ]

Where $s$ is the pixel skew factor (usually 0), and $f_x, f_y$ are the focal lengths represented in pixel units.

3. Depth of Field & Circle of Confusion (CoC)

In a real camera lens with an aperture, only objects at the exact Focus Distance ($D_f$) project perfectly onto the sensor as points. Other points project as blurred circles called the Circle of Confusion (CoC):

CoC = A * |S - D_f| / S * (f / (D_f - f))

Where $A$ is the aperture diameter ($A = f / N$), $S$ is the object distance, and $D_f$ is the lens focal plane distance.
